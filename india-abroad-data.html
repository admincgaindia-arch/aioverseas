Fewer people are leaving, not more

The number of Indian students departing for study abroad has fallen for two years running. This is the single most important number on this page, and most consultancy websites will not show it to you.

What the drop actually is

Departures fell 31% between 2023 and 2025 — from 9.08 lakh to 6.26 lakh. Canada saw the sharpest fall at around 41%, the UK about 27%.

But 6.26 lakh is still above the 2019 baseline of 5.86 lakh. The market did not collapse. It re-priced risk: the countries that tightened rules lost volume, and Germany, Ireland and New Zealand picked some of it up.

How many are being refused

This is the part nobody puts on a website. These are government figures, and they are the reason a well-built file matters more in 2026 than it did in 2022.

Canada

Indian study permit refusals, August 2025

74%

Up from 32% in August 2023. The overall refusal rate that month was 40%; Chinese applicants faced 24%. India now has the highest refusal rate of any country with more than 1,000 approved applicants.

Source: IRCC data cited by Reuters

Australia

Indian higher-education refusals, February 2026

40%

Against 3% for Chinese applicants. The overall offshore approval rate fell to 67.6%, the weakest in at least 21 years. India was moved to Evidence Level 3 — the highest scrutiny band — on 8 January 2026.

Source: Department of Home Affairs

United Kingdom

Indian student visa refusals, indicative

5%

Around 3–5% in recent reporting. In Q1 2025, 19,300 Indian main applicants were granted study visas, up 31% year on year. The pressure in the UK has been on dependants rather than on the student visa itself.

Source: Home Office reporting

What this means if you are applying now

Numbers are only useful if they change what you do. Here is what we take from them.

  • Country choice matters more than it used toA profile refused in Canada in 2025 might have cleared comfortably in Ireland or Germany the same year. Picking the route before picking the country is now the expensive mistake.
  • Funds are the first thing checkedCanada's published refusal reasons lead with financial insufficiency. Money that arrived recently reads as borrowed, whatever the balance says.
  • Fewer applicants, not fewer seatsIndian applications to Canada fell nearly 80% in two years. Universities still need students. A strong file today faces less competition than in 2023.
  • Fraud crackdowns hurt honest applicantsCanada flagged over 14,000 potentially fraudulent acceptance letters in one year. Every genuine applicant now pays for that in extra scrutiny — which is exactly why nothing false can go in your file.

Reading the numbers

What people ask us when they see these figures.

Why did so many fewer Indians go abroad in 2025?

Three policy changes landed at once: Canada capped study permits and tightened proof of funds, the UK removed dependant rights for most taught masters students, and the US saw a sharp fall in student visa issuance. None of these was aimed at India specifically, but India was the largest applicant group in all three, so India absorbed most of the impact.

Does a high refusal rate mean I should not apply?

No. It means preparation matters more than it used to. A 74% refusal rate also means one in four applications succeeded, and those were not random — they were files with seasoned funds, a course that matched the applicant's background, and documents that agreed with each other.

Which countries are getting easier?

Reporting through 2025 and 2026 shows Germany, Ireland, New Zealand and France absorbing demand redirected from the Big Four, helped by English-taught masters programmes and lower total cost. That does not make them easy — it makes them less crowded.

Are these numbers official?

The student population and departure figures come from India's Ministry of External Affairs and Bureau of Immigration, tabled in Parliament. The refusal figures come from Canadian and Australian government data reported by Reuters and Business Standard. Every source is linked at the bottom of this page so you can check it yourself.
