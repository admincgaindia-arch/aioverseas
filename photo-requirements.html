Visa photo rules,
country by country.

Half the photos rejected at visa centres are rejected for the same four reasons, and none of them is the size. Take this page to your photo studio.

Rules that apply almost everywhere

Get these six right and your photo will pass in most countries. Get one wrong and the application stops at the counter, which costs you an appointment slot, not just a reprint.

  • RecentTaken within the last six months, and it must look like you look now.
  • Neutral expressionMouth closed, no smile, both eyes open and clearly visible.
  • Straight to cameraHead square to the lens, not tilted or turned. Shoulders level.
  • No spectaclesSeveral countries now ban glasses outright. When in doubt, remove them — nobody is refused for not wearing glasses.
  • Head coveringPermitted for religious reasons, but the full face from chin to forehead must be uncovered.
  • No shadows or glareEven lighting, plain background, no shadow behind the head, no flash reflection.

Sizes below are the commonly applied specifications. Some posts and lodgement channels differ, and digital-upload requirements are not always the same as print requirements. Confirm on the official link for your destination before you pay a studio.

Where photos actually go wrong

Not the size. These four.

  • Shot against a cream wallLooks white to the eye, reads as off-colour on scan. Use a proper backdrop, not your living room.
  • Glasses left onThe most common single reason a photo is bounced at the visa centre counter.
  • Cropped from an old photoResizing a wedding photo never passes. Head proportion and resolution both fail.
  • Printed on glossy paperMatte finish is expected almost everywhere. Gloss causes glare on scanning.
