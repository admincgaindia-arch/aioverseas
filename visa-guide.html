Every visa route,
explained without the sales pitch.

Who actually qualifies, what you need to arrange, how the process really runs — and the unglamorous reasons files get refused. Read it before you pay anyone, including us.

Rules and thresholds change. Everything below is the general shape of each route, not the current legal text. Confirm the specifics on the official link given for each country.

Pick your route

Six routes cover almost every case. Choose the one closest to what you want, and read the whole tab — the refusal section at the bottom is the part most people skip and later regret.

Study visa

For a full-time course at a recognised institution abroad. In most countries it also carries a post-study work right, which is why it is the most common first step for Indian applicants.

Who generally qualifies

  • An admission or offer letter from a recognised institution
  • An approved English test score, where the course requires one
  • Funds you can genuinely show for tuition plus living costs, held long enough to look real
  • Academic record consistent with the course you picked
  • A believable reason to return, or a lawful stay-back route

What the officer is really testing

Two things only: can you pay for this without working illegally, and are you a genuine student rather than someone using a course as a migration shortcut. Every document you file is evidence for one of those two questions.

Documents to arrange

  • Passport with adequate validity, all used pages
  • 10th, 12th and degree marksheets and certificates
  • English test score report within validity
  • Offer letter and tuition fee payment receipt
  • Statement of purpose
  • Six months of bank statements, bank stamped
  • Loan sanction letter, if funding is a loan
  • Sponsor affidavit plus sponsor income proof and ITRs
  • Property valuation or CA net-worth certificate
  • Medical from a panel clinic, where required
  • Written explanation for any study gap

How it runs

  1. Shortlist — course and country against your marks, budget and intake
  2. Apply — institution applications, then offer letter
  3. Pay and confirm — tuition deposit, and country-specific proof such as a GIC account or blocked account
  4. Build the file — SOP, financials, sponsor papers
  5. File the visa — online form, fee, biometrics, medical
  6. Decision — approval, refusal, or a request for more documents

Why study files actually get refused

  • Funds that appeared suddenly. A large deposit two weeks before filing reads as borrowed money. Season your funds for months, not days.
  • A course that does not follow your history. A commerce graduate applying for nursing needs an explanation, not silence.
  • A generic SOP. Copied templates are recognised instantly. It has to be your own reasons, your own numbers.
  • Sponsor with no documented income. An affidavit alone proves nothing without ITRs and bank flow behind it.
  • An undisclosed earlier refusal. This is the one that turns a weak case into a ban.

Work permit

For a specific job abroad. Most routes need an employer who is licensed or approved to sponsor foreign workers — the job offer alone is not enough.

Who generally qualifies

  • A genuine job offer from an employer authorised to sponsor
  • Qualification or trade certification matching the role
  • Documented experience in the same or a related occupation
  • English ability at the level the route requires
  • Clean travel and police record

What the officer is really testing

Whether the job is real, whether the employer is entitled to hire you, and whether you can actually do the work. Fake experience letters fail here more often than anywhere else, because employers get contacted.

Documents to arrange

  • Passport and photographs to specification
  • Updated CV in the destination format
  • Job offer or employment contract
  • Sponsorship or labour-market reference number, where the route uses one
  • Experience letters on letterhead with role, dates and duties
  • Salary slips and Form 16 for the claimed years
  • Trade certificate or skill assessment result
  • Police clearance certificate
  • Medical examination report

How it runs

  1. Verify the employer — before you sign anything or pay anyone
  2. Skill assessment — where the occupation needs one
  3. Sponsorship step — employer files their side
  4. Build the file — experience, salary and identity evidence
  5. File the visa — form, fee, biometrics, medical, police clearance
  6. Decision and joining — travel dates around the employer's start date

Why work files actually get refused

  • An employer who cannot legally sponsor. Check this first. Many "offers" sold in India fail at this line.
  • Experience letters that do not match salary records. If Form 16 and the letter disagree, the letter loses.
  • Duties written vaguely. Officers match duties to occupation codes. "Handled operations" tells them nothing.
  • Money paid to an agent for a job. In several countries this is illegal and it sinks the application.

Permanent residence

Points-based systems. You enter a pool, you are ranked, and invitations go to the top scorers or to occupations the country currently wants. Nobody controls the cut-off.

What earns points almost everywhere

  • Age — younger scores higher, and it drops as you cross the bands
  • Education — assessed for equivalence, not just claimed
  • English or French ability — usually the cheapest points to buy with effort
  • Skilled work experience, documented properly
  • Job offer, provincial or state nomination, or a relative already settled
  • Spouse's education, language and experience, on many routes

What the officer is really testing

Whether every point you claimed can be proved. PR refusals are rarely about eligibility — they are about a claim you could not evidence when asked.

Documents to arrange

  • Passport and family identity documents
  • Educational credential assessment or equivalency report
  • Language test result within validity
  • Detailed experience letters with hours per week and duties
  • Salary slips, bank credits and Form 16 for each claimed job
  • Proof of settlement funds, seasoned for the required period
  • Marriage certificate and spouse documents
  • Police clearance for every country you lived in
  • Medicals for every family member

How it runs

  1. Score honestly — calculate before spending on anything
  2. Fix the gaps — retest English, get the credential assessment, document experience
  3. Enter the pool — expression of interest or profile creation
  4. Wait for an invitation — this is the part nobody can promise
  5. File the full application — usually a tight deadline once invited
  6. Verification and landing — medicals, police checks, then the move

Why PR files actually get refused

  • Points claimed but not provable. An experience letter without hours per week can cost you the whole claim.
  • Funds not seasoned. Transferred in a week before filing does not count as settlement funds.
  • A gap in police clearances. Every country you lived in, not only the last one.
  • Waiting to be perfect. Age points fall with every birthday. Filing at a workable score often beats waiting for an ideal one.

Visitor & family visit

Short stays — tourism, family visits, a graduation, a wedding. The lowest-cost visa and, for Indian applicants, one of the most refused. Almost always for the same two reasons.

Who generally qualifies

  • A clear, specific purpose with dates
  • Enough money for the trip, from a traceable source
  • Strong reasons to come back — job, business, family, property
  • A host who is lawfully in that country, if you are visiting someone
  • Travel history helps, but its absence is not fatal

What the officer is really testing

One question: will you leave. Everything else is secondary. A visitor file is an argument about your life in India, not about the destination.

Documents to arrange

  • Passport and photographs
  • Cover letter stating purpose and exact duration
  • Invitation letter from the host plus their status proof
  • Six months of personal bank statements
  • ITR acknowledgements for two or three years
  • Leave letter from employer, or business registration proof
  • Property papers and other ties to India
  • Travel itinerary and accommodation plan
  • Travel insurance for the stay

How it runs

  1. Fix the dates and purpose — vague plans read as weak intent
  2. Assemble ties evidence — this is the actual work
  3. Host documents — invitation, status proof, and their financials if they are sponsoring
  4. File and pay — online form, biometrics at the centre
  5. Decision — some posts also interview

Why visitor files actually get refused

  • Weak ties to India. Unemployed, unmarried, no property, no business — you must document whatever ties you do have, not hope they are assumed.
  • Money that is not yours. A friend's balance transferred in for show is the single most common cause.
  • A trip that does not make sense. A modest income with a three-week luxury itinerary invites doubt.
  • Relatives who overstayed. If a family member breached status abroad, address it rather than hoping it is not noticed.

Spouse & dependant

Joining a partner or parent who already holds a study, work or residence status. Rules here have tightened in several countries, so the route depends heavily on the principal's status.

Who generally qualifies

  • A genuine, subsisting relationship you can evidence over time
  • A principal whose visa category permits dependants — several student categories no longer do
  • Income or funds at the level that route requires
  • Accommodation arrangements, where the route asks for them

What the officer is really testing

Whether the relationship is real and continuing. Not the wedding — the years around it. Officers look for a life documented over time, not a folder assembled last month.

Documents to arrange

  • Marriage certificate and registration
  • Wedding photographs across events, with family present
  • Chat, call and travel history over a period
  • Joint bank account, insurance nominee, or shared address proof
  • Principal's visa, enrolment or employment letter
  • Principal's payslips and bank statements
  • Children's birth certificates, if applicable
  • Accommodation proof abroad

How it runs

  1. Confirm the principal's category allows dependants — check this before anything else
  2. Build relationship evidence — spread across time, not one week
  3. Meet the financial requirement — usually the principal's income or savings
  4. File together or after — depending on the route
  5. Biometrics and decision

Why dependant files actually get refused

  • Evidence created all at once. Photos from a single day and a fresh joint account look assembled, because they were.
  • The principal's category does not allow it. Several student routes now exclude dependants entirely.
  • Income below the threshold. Check the current figure — these move.
  • Inconsistent answers. If the two of you describe the marriage differently, that is the refusal.

Business & investor

For proprietors, directors and exporters travelling for meetings, trade fairs and negotiations — and for those setting up or investing in an overseas entity.

Who generally qualifies

  • A running business with filed accounts, not a shell registered last month
  • A specific commercial reason for the trip, with a counterpart abroad
  • Funds proportionate to the business, from business sources
  • For investor routes, capital you can trace lawfully to its origin

What the officer is really testing

Whether the business is genuine and whether the trip serves it. Source of funds matters more here than the amount — unexplained capital is the fastest way to a refusal.

Documents to arrange

  • Incorporation certificate, GST and MSME registration
  • Audited financials and ITRs for three years
  • Board resolution or partnership authorisation to travel
  • Invitation letter from the overseas counterpart
  • Trade fair or conference registration proof
  • Import export code and sample trade invoices
  • Company bank statements for six months
  • Business profile and product catalogue
  • For investor routes, a documented source-of-funds trail

How it runs

  1. Establish the business — filings, accounts, banking history in order
  2. Document the purpose — counterpart invitation or event registration
  3. Prepare the financial trail — company and personal, matching each other
  4. File the visa — business visitor, or the investor route if setting up
  5. Set-up steps abroad — entity registration, banking, licences, where applicable

Why business files actually get refused

  • A company with no trading history. Registration is not operation.
  • Financials that contradict the bank account. Turnover on paper with no matching flow reads as manufactured.
  • An invitation from a party with no visible business. The counterpart gets checked too.
  • Untraceable capital on investor routes. Cash and undocumented gifts fail source-of-funds review.

Country by country

The routes each country runs, and the official page to verify current rules. Bookmark the official link — it is the only source that is never out of date.

Canada

Routes: Study permit with a post-graduation work permit pathway, Express Entry for federal skilled routes, Provincial Nominee streams, spouse open work permit, visitor visa and the parent super visa.

Distinctive: a points system that changes with each draw, and provincial streams that can invite at scores well below the federal cut-off. Proof-of-funds and credential assessment rules are strict.

Official: Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada

Australia

Routes: Subclass 500 student visa, 485 temporary graduate stream, 189 skilled independent, 190 state nominated, 491 regional, and employer-sponsored routes.

Distinctive: the Genuine Student requirement makes your stated intent part of the assessment, and skills assessment by the relevant authority is usually mandatory before the points claim.

Official: Department of Home Affairs

United Kingdom

Routes: Student visa on a CAS from a licensed sponsor, the Graduate route for stay-back, Skilled Worker with a sponsoring employer, standard visitor and business visitor.

Distinctive: everything runs on sponsor licences and the CAS. Dependant rules for students have tightened, and salary thresholds on the work route move — check the current figure before planning.

Official: GOV.UK visas and immigration

Germany

Routes: Public university admission, blocked account funding, the Opportunity Card for job seekers, EU Blue Card for skilled employment, and Ausbildung vocational training.

Distinctive: tuition at public universities is minimal, so the cost barrier is the blocked account rather than fees. Document attestation and translation requirements are heavier than most countries.

Official: Make it in Germany

New Zealand

Routes: Student visa, Accredited Employer Work Visa, and the Skilled Migrant residence pathway.

Distinctive: the work route runs entirely through employer accreditation, so employer verification is the first real step, not the last.

Official: Immigration New Zealand

United States

Routes: F-1 student visa on an I-20 with SEVIS payment, B1/B2 visitor and business, and employment categories that run through employers.

Distinctive: the interview decides it. Documents support your answers but do not replace them, and appointment availability often drives the timeline more than processing does.

Official: US Department of State — Visas

Ireland

Routes: Student visa for third-level programmes, Stamp 1G stay-back after graduation, and the Critical Skills Employment Permit.

Distinctive: the critical skills occupation list drives the work route, so eligibility depends on whether your role is on it.

Official: Irish Immigration Service

UAE & Gulf

Routes: Employment visa through a sponsoring employer, Golden Visa for qualifying categories, freezone company setup with an investor visa, visit and transit visas.

Distinctive: fastest turnaround of any destination here, but status is tied to the employer or the entity. Attestation of educational documents is a mandatory step people routinely underestimate.

Official: UAE Government portal

Six mistakes that cost people the most

These are route-independent. We see them every week, and every one of them is avoidable before you file.

Paying before the assessment

Money goes to an agent, then the profile turns out not to fit the route sold. Get the honest read first — it should cost nothing.

Arranged bank balances

A borrowed balance parked for two weeks is visible in the statement pattern. It is the leading cause of financial refusals, and it can be treated as misrepresentation.

Hiding an earlier refusal

Refusals are visible across systems and often shared between countries. Disclosing one is survivable. Hiding it is what triggers multi-year bans.

Signing an offer nobody verified

Employers must be licensed to sponsor in most countries. Verify the licence before you resign from your current job.

Handing over original documents

No consultant needs to keep your originals. Scans are enough. Where an embassy needs a physical original, insist on a receipt.

Believing a guarantee

No consultant, anywhere, can guarantee a visa. A guarantee usually means someone is planning to file something false in your name.

The words everyone uses and nobody explains

If a consultant uses one of these and cannot explain it in one sentence, that is worth noticing.

SOPStatement of Purpose. Your written explanation of why this course, this country, and what you do after. Written by you, not downloaded.
CASConfirmation of Acceptance for Studies. The UK reference number a licensed university issues before you can file the student visa.
I-20 & SEVISThe US admission document and the student tracking fee you pay before the visa interview.
GICGuaranteed Investment Certificate. A Canadian account holding your first year of living costs, released to you monthly after arrival.
Blocked accountThe German equivalent. Funds locked in an account, released in fixed monthly amounts once you land.
ECAEducational Credential Assessment. An independent body confirms your Indian degree equals the destination's standard. Needed for most PR routes.
CRSComprehensive Ranking System. Canada's points score in the Express Entry pool. Draw cut-offs move constantly.
PGWPPost-Graduation Work Permit. Canada's stay-back after a qualifying programme, usually the bridge from study to PR.
LMIALabour Market Impact Assessment. A Canadian employer's proof that hiring you does not displace a local worker.
PNPProvincial Nominee Program. A Canadian province nominates you, which can invite at a much lower score than the federal draw.
Skills assessmentAn Australian authority verifies your qualification and experience against the occupation you are claiming, before you claim points.
Stamp 1GIreland's post-study permission to remain and look for work.
BiometricsFingerprints and photograph, given at a visa application centre. A separate appointment from filing.
VFSA private company several countries use to collect applications and biometrics. It processes paperwork; it does not decide anything.
AttestationOfficial stamping of your documents to make them valid abroad. Mandatory for the Gulf and heavy for Germany.
MisrepresentationGiving false information or hiding a material fact. Typically a multi-year ban from that country, and it follows you elsewhere.
Ties to home countryJob, business, property, dependants — the evidence that you have reasons to come back. The core of every visitor visa.
Seasoned fundsMoney that has sat in the account long enough to be believable. Recent deposits are the most common financial red flag.
